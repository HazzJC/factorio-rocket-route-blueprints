Rocket Route Blueprints

Pick a planet and a raw material. Get one blueprint book: a Nauvis supply sender, a launch outpost, and a receiver on Nauvis or in Nauvis orbit. The outpost launches when the rocket is full and calls for supplies on its own.

How the loop runs
1. Ore fills two warehousesBelts feed two storage warehouses (north and east of the pad) through filtered inserters. The pad loads from them, so mining never stops while a rocket is away. When all 500 slots are full and the rocket is fuelled, it launches to the receiver.
2. Outpost counts stockLiquid fuel (plus unconverted fuel items), sections (loose and packed) and capsules are added up on one red network. Fuel wanted = launches in stock x launch fuel; the shortfall divided by the liquid per fuel item is the number of items to request.
3. AAI sends the requestOnly what is missing is asked for: fuel items (exact count, up to one rocket), a packed-section lot and a capsule when those run below the reserve.
4. Nauvis loads and shootsInserters load only what was asked for from the requester chest. When the load matches, a decider fires the launch and the supply rocket flies to the outpost dock.

Each rocket brings its own capsule and most of its sections back to the dock it lands at, so a route that lands one supply rocket per ore launch stays in balance by itself. If supply rockets outnumber ore launches (a refill that needs more than one rocket), three valve inserters on the outpost warehouse push the surplus capsules, sections and packed sections into the next ore rocket, so stock never piles up; the receiver's parts chest sends them home. Space Exploration only launches to an empty dock, so a warehouse drains the dock after every landing.

Setup checklist
  1. Place the receiver first so its dock name exists. For orbit, choose a clear patch of Nauvis orbit away from asteroids; scaffold tiles are in the blueprint.
  2. Place the outpost. Open its launch pad and read "fuel required"; put that number in Launch fuel needed and copy the book again. For a space outpost choose a clear patch of orbit or asteroid belt.
  3. Place the Nauvis sender. Pipe liquid rocket fuel into the buffer tank (pipe stub on the west side). It needs the fuel for its own launch to the outpost too. If you already have an older sender of this route, deconstruct it first: pasting a blueprint over an existing one keeps its old wires, and an old wire between the AAI receiver and the pad network makes older versions run away (the request numbers climb every tick and the pad loads dozens of capsules). This version cannot run away, but its link panel says STALE WIRE when it finds one.
  4. Connect power to one substation per blueprint. The AAI sender and receiver stop working when under-powered.
  5. Run ore onto the four outpost belts: two from the west along the north side, two from the south along the east side. Cover the Nauvis requester chest, the refinery modules and the receiver provider chests with roboports.
  6. Nothing needs naming by hand: pad names, launch triggers, destinations and the AAI channel are stored in the blueprint tags.
Reading the lamps and panels (what is it waiting on?)
  • Outpost pad panel (south of the pad, with a red and a yellow lamp): reads the pad itself. First true message wins: no destination, waiting on fuel (the pad tank is below one launch), waiting on pad (its 100 sections + capsule), waiting on ore, ready. "Ready" with no launch means the receiver dock is not empty or a rocket is still inbound. A second stock panel next to it reads everything the outpost holds: pile-up, no capsule, under 100 sections, fuel below the reserve.
  • Four small request panels (right of it): one per item (fuel, packed sections, capsules, mining fluid). Yellow ASKING NAUVIS = the outpost is waiting for that item to arrive; green = covered. A red panel below says when the fuel request has hit the one-rocket limit.
  • Valve panel (west): says which surplus (capsules, sections, packed) is being sent home. Supply dock panel and NET SPLIT panel (below the dock): the dock still holds cargo (warehouse full?) or the dock side cannot see the logic block.
  • Receiver: dock holds cargo, ore chest full, parts chest nearly full. If any stays lit the outpost is waiting: something has to consume the ore. Launches only happen to an empty dock.
  • Nauvis sender: a pad panel (waiting on liquid fuel, own sections, capsule, loaded and waiting, loading, idle), a requester-chest panel (which item the bots have not delivered) and a link panel beside the AAI receiver (it also reports STALE WIRE: an old wire joining the receiver to the pad). Both ends of a route must come from the same book (route key).
Things worth knowing
  • Productivity modules in the refineries raise the liquid fuel per fuel item (the blueprint requests the modules; the tool shows the bonus and the speed you give up). The outpost converts stock and requests with the same figure, so the number of fuel items it asks for falls as productivity rises.
  • The receiver sorts ore from everything else. Ore goes to a passive-provider warehouse and the east belt; recovered sections, capsules and the valve surplus go to an active-provider storehouse so they flow into your storage and can be reused. If the ore is not consumed the dock stays full and the next launch waits.
  • Cargo scales the cost: a half-empty rocket burns less fuel and fewer sections, so a small supply load is cheaper than a full one.
  • Ores that need a fluid to mine (iridium, naquium and uranium need sulfuric acid) get a second supply line. Barrels: filled barrels ride in the supply rocket, an assembler unbarrels them into a tank, empties fly home. Sulfur + plates: sulfur, iron plates (and optionally water ice) ride in the supply rocket and chemical plants make acid on site (planet outposts only). Either way you connect your drills to the pipe stub on the acid tank.
  • Space Exploration does not allow ordinary belts, pipes or assemblers in space, so the orbit receiver and the space outpost use space belts, space pipes and a space assembler, on scaffold tiles that are part of the blueprint.
